# Tiago Jerónimo Silva · things I’ve made

A personal site in three pages: the front page, with everything I have made on it, a card each; a plain CV at `/cv/`; and a small voxel game at `/game/`.

React + TypeScript + Vite. The front page and the CV are plain pages (a few kilobytes of their own on top of React); only the game loads three.js, through react-three-fiber.

```bash
npm install
npm run dev      # http://localhost:5173
npm run build    # type-check and build to dist/
npm test         # checks the list of projects and the game's maps, and plays whole games
```

## The front page

A grid of cards: games, web things and the places I have worked, and under a dashed line of its own the old stuff (`old` on a project: a festival film, a YouTube channel, some Photoshop montages, some islands made of cubes, and a video of voxels in Unity). A chip for each kind filters both. A click on a card opens it in a sheet (a native `<dialog>`) with the rest of the words and the links; a video plays in the sheet, and nothing is fetched from YouTube until play is pressed. The address says which card is open (`/#curvatron`), so a card can be linked to and the back button shuts it.

To add a project, add an entry to the list in `src/home/projects.ts`. If it has a picture, put it in `public/thumbs/` and name it in `picture`; without one the card shows a small drawing, twelve pixels by nine, from `src/home/art.ts`. More pictures of the same thing go in `more`, and are shown small in a row in its sheet, where a click on one shows it big. The cards stand newest first, by the years in `when` (the wide one leads whatever its years, and what has no year comes last). The "Work" cards are made from the chapters of the CV in `src/content.ts`, so those words live in one place. `npm test` checks that every card has an address of its own, something to show and somewhere to lead, and that a picture it names is there.

A link that leaves the site opens in a tab of its own (`outside` in `src/content.ts`), so the site stays where it was. The Email link also shows the address itself under the cursor, with a button that copies it. The pictures in `public/thumbs/` so far: Curvatron's, from the studio's own site; the studio's logo; the still YouTube shows for the festival film, with its black bars cut off; Snap Marker's screenshot from its README; one of the Photoshop montages; three voxel pictures of mine from about 2019; the still YouTube shows for the Unity video, with its black bars cut off; the small island awake, for the poster to turn into; and the same island as the game starts, dry, twice: at the shape of an ordinary card (`small` on a project) and wide (`picture`, for the sheet and the wide card). The wide card is a poster: it writes the project's name in big letters on the left and keeps the picture to the right, on the colour the picture has round its island. It also carries the project's first link as a button under the name (Play), so that the game is one click away. That colour is written into `home.css` (`.wide .shot`), so another picture there wants it changed to match. Under the cursor the island wakes: a second picture of the same shape (`after` on a project: here the same small island from the same place, green, with none of the land that comes up later in the game) fades in over the first, and the ground of the poster turns to that picture's colour, which is written into the stylesheet beside the other; on a screen with no cursor it comes and goes by itself. A picture marked `framed` is shown smaller, on a white card with round corners and a small hard shadow, like the marks of the places: for a picture that gains nothing by being big. The Work cards also carry the marks of the places, small, in the corner of their pictures (`logos` on a chapter, files in `public/logos/`: the places' own icons, so that a card is told at a glance for the real place it is). A Work card may also show the place's own public site in place of its drawing (`shot` on a chapter), as it was when I worked there: the top of sign.com as it is now, and the front pages of Nomoko's platform (2022), Drover (2020) and Runtime Revolution (2019) from the web archive's copies. They are my own screenshots, shown small on a white card, and all of one shape (16 by 9, like every picture shown that way), so that their cards are of one size. The words on the cards are in the voice of the game; those of the Work cards are the CV's, with one small aside at the end (`aside` on a chapter in `src/content.ts`), which the CV page leaves out.

The page has a few things to play with, none of them in the way of the cards (all in `src/home/Home.tsx` and `home.css`; whoever has asked their device for less movement gets the page without any of it that moves by itself):

- **The moon**, a small face at the end of the links, brings the night (and is a sun while it lasts): the page goes dark, the cards keep their lights on, and fireflies drift over it from top to foot. They belong to the page, so scrolling goes past them. Night is a mark on the page (`data-night`) that the styles go by, and it is remembered.
- **Three at the foot of the page**, a bunny, a fox and a broccoli: they blink, a pat makes one jump, one tilt its head and one wobble, each with a word of its own, and by night they are asleep.
- **Three snails** hide on the page: one looks over the top of a card, one walks the dashed line over the old stuff, one sits in a sheet (`BEHIND` and `INSIDE` say which). A click finds one: it jumps out of its hiding place and is gone, and the foot of the page counts it. They are not remembered: at the next visit all three hide again.
- **The drawings move** while the cursor is on their card, a pixel at a time: the cube that hangs over its tablet, the worm that leans out of its apple one way and the other, ducks in and peeps over the top. Every pixel of a drawing carries its ink as a class (`i-K`), which is how the styles pick out an eye or a flame.
- **A stamp** goes on every card that has been opened, and the foot of the page counts them. Like the snails, they are forgotten at the next visit.
- **Two visitors** come by now and then, each in its own time and to whichever card it likes: a raccoon comes along the top of one in a few bounds, has a sleep there and goes (a click wakes it, and it is off at once), and a llama walks past behind one, as much of it as shows over the top. Nothing a visitor to the page does brings them. The three at the foot of the page stay where they are.
- **Stickers**: the marks of the places, and the snails once found, come off under the pointer and stay on the page wherever they are let go (`lift` in `Home.tsx`), until the page is loaded again.
- **The cards** come in one after another, slide to their new places when a chip is pressed (a view transition, where the browser has them), and the sheet grows out of the card that was clicked.

The last card of the grid, **Say hello**, is no project: it says what work I am looking for and how to reach me (`hello` in `projects.ts`).

It is the first page anybody sees, so it is kept light. The poster's picture is asked for from `index.html` itself, before the script that draws the page has run; pictures that are shown small have a small copy beside them (`-s`, made by hand with `sips`: `npm test` checks they are there), and the browser takes whichever is enough; the poster's second picture is not fetched until a few seconds in; everything below the first screen waits until it is scrolled to. On a slow phone connection the page shows after about a second and its biggest picture half a second later, with about 320 KB loaded in all.

The CV at `/cv/` is the same chapters, newest first, as one column of plain words that prints as it reads. What `src/content.ts` says comes from my CV and my reference letter, or from me directly (the job title is the one I give on LinkedIn).

## The game at `/game/`

"Wake the island" is a tiny click-to-explore game on its own page (`game/index.html`, code in `src/game/`). The island starts dry. Water wakes the ground within a few tiles of it: grass, flowers and leaves grow there. A handful of things hold the water back or the life in, and finding them is the whole game. Some of them make more land rise out of the sea, each isle with its own look, its own rule and its own things to find:

| Isle | Raised by | What wakes it | Things to find |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The island | there from the start | water | boulder on the spring, a beaver asleep by its dam (two knocks: the first gets a mutter out of it), rain cloud, thorns on the old tree, capped well, crate with a beehive, seed pod, caged bird |
| The meadow | the caged bird | water, and bunnies | a tortoise asleep in the stream bed, a rabbit hole with fresh dirt round it, three sleeping stones, a windmill tied down by ivy |
| The snow | the sleeping stones | warmth and meltwater | a penguin frozen into the pond, a fire pit under snow, a plugged geyser |
| The mountain | the geyser (a plank bridge joins it to the meadow) | a lake behind an ice dam | a snowball on the summit, a bell (it wakes the marmots, and only them), a mole |
| The swamp | the windmill | rain, and light at night | a frog that calls rain, a fairy ring to tap from smallest to tallest, a lantern that brings the night |
| The beach | the river reaching the sea | the tide | a shell that calls the tide, a buried crab, a cactus that needs the frog's rain |
| The volcano | the tide going out | lava and ash | a boulder stuck in the crater; its lava builds a strip of new land, and warms the egg in the ash, which then opens at a knock (never by itself) |
| The garden | the bees from the crate (a plank bridge joins it to the island) | a watering can | the watering can, a chicken coop barred shut |
| The islet | the first low tide (stepping stones lead to it from the beach) | by itself | nothing that has to be found: a buried chest, and a lighthouse whose lamp turns at night and throws a pool of light round and round |

Drag moves the view, the right button (or a two-finger twist) turns it, the wheel (or a pinch) zooms; on the keyboard the arrow keys or W A S D slide it, Q and E turn it, and + and − zoom (on the middle of the view: where the cursor lies has no say in what a key does). A key pressed along with ⌘ or Ctrl is left to the browser, and no key counts as held once the window has lost the keyboard. The camera never comes back by itself: wherever it goes, it stays, and the view is the player's from there. When new land comes up it goes to see it (the controls wait until it has arrived, about a second). It goes along with whatever a knock sends on its way: the freed bird out over the sea, the snowball down the mountain, the seeds on the wind from the windmill, the frog's cloud, a fish on its way to the penguin, and whatever else a wish sends over: the carrot to the bunnies, the branch to the beaver, the coconut to the crab, the pearl to the dragon, the firefly to the frog (`trail` in `Island.ts`). From far off it comes in to do so, near enough to see the thing; it is never brought nearer than it already was; and it stays where the journey ends. Water and lava, which run every way at once, are followed the older way: while they travel out of view the camera shifts and widens just enough to keep them in the picture together with what was being looked at, and stays out there when things have settled. Moving the view yourself ends either. And from far off, the knock that finishes a thing brings the view in for a look at what comes of it. It waits a beat first, to see what that is: where it runs off (water down a bed, a bird out to sea) the view stays wide or goes along; and it is not while the camera is off seeing new land. Once it has set out it goes all the way: it used to be called off by the first of the water, halfway, which looked like a mistake. And a view that has been zoomed in on stays on what it shows when new land comes up: only a view of the whole world grows along with the world. The same goes for a window that is made another shape: a close view keeps its distance, and it is the whole world that is fitted to the window afresh. How near the view may come is a fixed distance, not a multiple of the whole-world view, so it is the same in a narrow window as in a wide one. The buttons sit together in the top right corner: a loudspeaker for the sound, the journal (the map and all that goes with it), a tip, and a cog. The journal's button shows a dot when something has gone into its log without being said out loud (those lines carry the same dot in the log the next time it is opened), and the tip's when nothing has been found for a minute. Under the journal's word runs a thin line that fills as the things are found. All of them count, those on land still under the sea too, so it only ever goes forward; resting the cursor on the button gives the numbers. They stand on a patch of the page's colour, so that they can be read with land behind them. The cog opens a small sheet, in plain words (this is where people look when they are lost, so no jokes): under *Game*, two buttons of one size and nothing beside them, *New game* (see Shuffle below: another island, and the one that is left is not kept) and *Postcard* (see below), with what each does in full on its tooltip, and a line of small print saying that the game saves by itself and that a new game replaces the island; under *Controls*, a small table with a row for each thing there is to do (knock and pat, move, zoom, turn, the next line of text) and across it the ways of doing it, with the mouse and with keys, each under its heading, or with fingers, whichever the screen has (`HELP` in `GamePage.tsx`); and under *About me*, one line saying whose game this is, with a link to the front page of the site and one to the CV (the same line stands at the foot of the card at the end); a click beside the open sheet only shuts it and does not reach the world. A moment after an island has come up, three small lines in the top left corner say how to move, zoom and turn the view (`HINTS` in `GamePage.tsx`, worded for a mouse or for fingers; where there is a keyboard the keys that do the same come after, in brackets: `Drag to move [or W A S D]`). Each goes away once that has been done on that island, the ones left close up, and a finished game shows none. Which have been done is kept with the island's notes, so a new game shows all three again and an island come back to does not. Where there is a mouse, a small label by the cursor says what it is resting on, and whatever can be pointed at has one, the things that are only scenery included (the rowing boat, the plank bridges, the stepping stones, the slide, the windmill itself); the label stays until the cursor moves, since animals do not hold still. A label says what is so now: the shell says what its next call will do, which depends on where the tide is; a burrow is empty once the bell has had its marmot up; the stones round the egg are round where an egg was once it has hatched; the sandcastle is finished once it has its flag. And what has been found and stays on show keeps a label for afterwards (the open well, the old tree, the fire, the bell, the empty nest, the open chests, the tipped watering can): whatever answers a click says what it is. `npm test` plays whole islands and fails if a label is left saying what was, or if something that answers a click has none. Nothing under a button or an open card is pointed at: no label, no click and no zoom go through the map. The picture fills the window from edge to edge, and the page behind it is kept the exact colour the empty part of the picture has on the screen (`toned` in `Island.ts` does the same sum the last step of drawing does), so that whatever a browser shows of the page round the picture, or tints with its colour, runs on from it without a seam.

Most things take two or three knocks. Every knock that does not break a thing leaves a mark that stays (a crack, a bar gone, a piece fallen off, a small "!" over somebody asleep), and the noise of it floats up from the thing as a word. Until something has been found, the boulder on the spring stirs every few seconds (two small jolts, a fifth bigger for a moment), so that whoever has not clicked on anything yet sees where to begin; after the first find, whatever it was, it is left in peace. Which thing that is, the isle's file says (`start` on a thing). And all along, the things still to be found stir a little now and then: one at a time, a small jolt, and only those that are in the picture (`STIR` and `twitch` in `Island.ts`); somebody asleep stirs in its sleep. Only what counts towards finishing and answers a touch: the things that take a knock, and the fairy ring by way of its five mushrooms (`touched` on a thing). The cactus does not, since it wants rain and not a finger; nor does scenery, a toy or a secret, ever. On a touch screen, where no cursor turns into a hand and no label comes up, that is all that says what can be touched. The Tip button takes the view to one thing still to be found, leaves it there, and stands an arrow over the thing. When the things are all found it goes on to the snails, and after them to the secrets, each with its riddle. The game is kept in the browser between visits, one per island: it is put back by doing every knock again, fast and without the show (`replay` in `Island.ts`); *New game*, under the cog or on the card at the end, throws it away.

The words are in one voice throughout: dry, aware that it is a game, fond of the things in it. Every line that points at something to do still says what to do; the aside comes after. A secret or a combo is named on the map by the first sentence of what is said when it is found, so that sentence stays plain.

Night, which the lantern in the swamp turns on and off, is not only a colour: fireflies come out, and a few of those in view carry a small real light (six at most, three on a phone, since lights are dear to draw), animals of the day curl up and sleep where they are, owls and bats are out, a lit fire pit draws the birds and the penguin to sleep round it (in a ring, wherever there is open ground at its level: `around` in `Island.ts`; a pit at the top of a cliff gets half a ring), and now and then a star falls that can be caught. The shell on the beach, once it has called the tide in, sends it out and calls it back: low tide empties the lagoon, and leaves starfish on its floor and stepping stones out to the islet. Grass and flowers lean away from the cursor, and fish come to see what it is. When the whole world is awake a balloon comes over, and a click on it gets the thank-you.

None of the following is needed to finish, and each has its own counter:

- **Combos (8):** things that only happen when two other things are both true, in whatever order they came about. Wind and the seed pod, bees and fruit trees, bees and the flowering cactus, rain on the fire pit, rain on lava, night and the lagoon, night and the windmill, the bell at night.
- **Small things that answer a click:** a secret or a combo found shows where it is, with a ring of gold going up and the word for it; the chests open (the lid goes up and back, and coins jump out); the well takes a coin and splashes at every click once it has water in it; the windmill stops at a click, and the wind with it, and starts again at the next; the dragon's fireworks last a dozen seconds and a pat sets them off again; the fire in the fire pit is four small flames one on another, with a tip that sways. And the well is always dealt a place at least four tiles from any river, spring or pond, so that the ground it wakes is ground nothing else has reached.
- **Secrets (16):** a four-leaf clover, a wishing well, a squirrel in the old tree, a blue egg in a nest on top of a bare trunk, a sandcastle, a ghost in the swamp at night, the scarecrow's lodger, a cave full of crystals and bats on the far side of the mountain (turn the world round), the chest on the islet, a falling star caught, the frog's song played back on the fairy ring (once the frog has its night-light it sings five notes, in the colours of the ring's five caps; the tune is different on every island), all seven snails, a snowman (three pats on a heap of snow; the penguin gets a slide out of it), and three that come out of wishes.
- **Bottle post:** while there is anything nobody has found, a bottle washes up on the beach now and then with a clue: a riddle for a secret or a combo, or the clue of a thing still to be found. A riddle stays next to its secret or combo on the map until it is found.
- **Snails (7):** one hides on each of the seven isles, by preference behind a tree. They are blue until found, and red after.
- **Wishes:** every animal's first wish is a pat. Six of them have a second: a minute or so after one is free, a small bubble comes up over its head with a picture of what it would like (one bubble at a time, twenty seconds apart at least, three at most; a click on it says the wish, and the friend book has it in words). The thing is somewhere else in the world and sparkles now and then, a click sends it over, and the animal gives something back.
- **The map:** one button, four tabs. *Map* starts with the title, the bar that says how much of the world is alive, and the counts (they used to sit in a card in the corner of the picture; the picture now has the whole window to itself), and under them a chart of the world from above, a pixel a tile, as it is at that moment: only what has come up out of the sea is on it, with the bridges between isles, a name on each, and a rubber stamp on every isle where everything is found. The list under it has a row for every isle: those still under the sea as question marks, so that it shows there is more without saying what. *Friends* has a face for each (a dark shape until met), its next wish or, once it has had them all, a line on what became of it; a name takes the camera to that friend and keeps it there until the view is moved. *Finds* is a sticker album of the secrets (dark shapes until found), with the snails and the combos. *Log* keeps everything that has been said on that island, each line once and the latest on top, the ending included. It reads in chapters: a piece of land coming up leaves a divider (*The garden came up*), and a tip whose thing has since been found goes dim and is marked *✓ done*, and a wish that has been granted *✓ granted*, so the ones still open stand out. In front of each line stands who or what it was about: a friend's face and name, or a word or two (the thing that was knocked on, *Tip* for a clue, *Sign*, *Bottle*, *Secret*, *Combo*, *About you* for what the game remarks on). Most of that follows from what was knocked on or patted when the line was set off (`Island.say`); where it cannot, the line says so itself. In front of the name is a face, or a small picture of the thing itself, drawn from the very boxes it is made of in the world as they were when it was knocked on (`sketch` in `sprites.ts`); failing both, a square of its colour. A name that leads somewhere is a button: a click shuts the map and takes the view there, close up (to a friend wherever it is by now, to a thing where it stands). A thing that is not there any more leads nowhere: once the cloud has rained itself out, or the boulder is in pieces, its name is still written in its colour but is no longer a button, in the log and in the line at the bottom alike (`gone` in the game's state). Where the view goes to look at something, it stays far enough back to take in all of it: the cloud hangs well above its hill, and from close to the hill it would be off the top of the picture (`within`). And a view that is brought in closer for a look (by a tip, by a name that was clicked, by the knock that finishes a thing, by new land) goes a touch too far and steps back by as much, about a twentieth: the step back is how one can tell that it has arrived (`STEP`). In a clue, the words that name the thing it is about are written in [square brackets] in the code (`'That [fat brown pod] looks ready to burst'`): the page writes them in the colour of the thing itself (made dark enough to read by day, light enough by night) wherever the line is shown, and a click on them goes to the thing, from the log and from the line at the bottom of the picture alike. The log is saved with the island, and an island saved before there was one gets it from being put back. The pictures are pixel art drawn from the same boxes the animals are made of in the world (`sprites.ts`), so they cannot drift apart; a few things are drawn by hand in the same file.
- **A postcard:** *Postcard* under the cog, or *A postcard of it* on the card at the end, makes a picture of the island as the camera sees it at that moment, on a card with the island's number, how awake it is, the knocks so far and the date, and a rubber stamp once the whole world is awake. The buttons and the words are not in the picture. It comes up to be looked at, with a button that saves it (`postcard` in `GamePage.tsx`).
- **A picture for links:** `public/wake-the-island.jpg` (1200 by 630) and the `og:` tags in `game/index.html` are what a link to the game shows where it is pasted, in a chat or on a social site. The tags give whole addresses, the site's on GitHub Pages (`https://tiagojeronimo.github.io/`): should the site ever move, they move with it. The picture was made by hand in the dev server: a finished island by day in a window of that size, the game's own canvas drawn onto a 2D canvas, the title written over it, saved as a JPEG.
- **A visitor, sometimes:** on about one visit in four somebody turns up who has nothing to do with anything: a capybara in the hot spring on the snow, or an axolotl in the bog of the swamp. Nothing counts it, and it may not be there next time.
- **What the bees bring out:** apples on the fruit trees (a combo); and, with nothing said about it, blossom on the old tree (a dozen small flowers here and there on it, white for the most part and some pink, like a cherry in spring) and oranges on some of the first island's other trees, about two in five of them. An orange tree answers a knock as an apple tree does: a leaf or two, and a fruit that lies where it fell until somebody near comes and eats it (`FLOWERS` and `fruit` in `tree`, `Island.ts`).
- **Small things:** the first duck has ducklings that follow in a line, birds sit on the scarecrow, the meadow's snail rides the tortoise once it has been found, and the geyser leaves a hot pool where the penguin sits at night.
- **Night:** the sky stays plain and dark, and the land stands in it as it does by day (a moon, stars and northern lights were tried, and taken out again: with stars round it the land looked as if it floated). Now and then a pale speck glitters on the sea near the land, a very little, and the rivers and ponds glint gold; the falling star is a small twinkle with a fine tail, and a click that passes close by it catches it. (There were already fireflies, glowing mushrooms, the lighthouse lamp and the birds at the fire.)
- **Music:** with the sound on, a small tune made in the browser gains a voice for every isle and gets brighter as the land wakes. The sound is off until somebody turns it on with its button: until that has been pressed once, a small label under it asks ("Sound?", for the first twenty seconds of a visit). It is off again at every visit, and asks again: the choice used to be remembered, but a browser lets a page make no sound before the first touch (and sometimes not after it), so the button said on and nothing played. Once it is on, every click, key or tap says so again, which wakes a sound the browser has sent to sleep (a finger lifting counts as well as a click). A phone set to silent stays silent. Small noises do not carry: the chicks cheep at every hop, and that is only heard from close by, with the view on them (`hears` in `Island.ts`). And every hop of a chick comes down on a beat of the tune: it waits for the right moment to leave the ground, and after it for a beat or a few (`untilBeat` in `music.ts`; with the sound off it is the same beat, counted on the game's clock).
- **Shuffle:** the coastlines are always the same; *New game*, under the cog, deals the loose things on every isle out afresh, along with where the snails hide and what the frog sings. The scenery follows the number too: the shape and height of every tree, which way a rock lies, and where grass and flowers come up. And every isle is drawn three times (`variants` on an isle: another map on the same coastline, with the same things on it; only the strip of new land the lava builds has one): a numbered island gets one of the three for each isle, each chosen by itself, so there are thousands of worlds before any shuffling. On the first island, for instance, `?seed=1` has the first map, `?seed=5` the second (the hill in the middle of the north shore, the river down the east side, the pond in the south-west) and `?seed=2` the third (the hill on the left, the river slanting to the south-west, the pond in the east). The number in the link (`?seed=1234`) always gives the same island, so one can be passed on; no number is the world as drawn. The dealing keeps to a few rules (`deal` in `grid.ts`): nothing worth finding right behind something tall, room for the sails of the windmill, a well away from the water, and no tree within two tiles of the old tree, which is a good deal bigger than the others and would grow into them (seeds do not fall there either). It tries up to a thousand hands before it leaves an isle as drawn.
- **The animals, left to themselves:** each kind has a few things it does with a quiet moment (a look round, a yawn, a scratch, a stretch; the duck goes head under, the goat grazes, birds preen), and those with a head apart from their body nod and turn it. They look at what happens: a knock near by, a pat somebody else got, the bell, a new isle coming up, a falling star, the balloon, the fireworks. And at each other: two that stand close exchange a look now and then, small ones on the ground hop away from under the dragon, a bird may take the tortoise for a perch and ride along, and a bird flies off a tree that is knocked. Bedtime starts with a yawn and morning with a stretch.
- **Water:** a click on water throws a flat stone. It lands where the click was and skips on along the water, with a small splash at every touch, and somewhere near by a fish jumps (not up north: the penguin's pond has none, which is the trouble with it). Swimmers keep to a tile each: two fish that come to see the same thing stop a tile apart. Where water goes over an edge, off the land or down a step, it falls as a stream of drops right across the edge, with a little mist where it lands; a fall that is out of the picture is not drawn, and one seen from close to gets more drops.
- **Signs:** a wooden sign stands at each of the six bridges. A click reads it: one of its three lines, and the next one the next time. They say nothing useful.
- **The game notices how it is played,** and says so, each thing once: five, ten and twenty knocks on something with nothing to give; poking the ground; hammering; a favourite who gets all the pats; coming back after a minute away; a third tip asked for; ten stones; a long night; never having turned the world round; and a thing that is not left alone (the lantern, the bell, the frog, the shell, a sign) has a line of its own for the sixth time. A remark waits its turn, so it never lands on top of a line that has only just come up. Coming back to a saved island is greeted according to how long it has been. The card at the end lists up to four true things about how the island was played. What is noticed is kept per island next to the saved game (`wake-the-island/3/<seed>/notes`), and goes when the island is started over.
- **Being tried out** (each is easy to take out again; the names are the ones to ask by):
  - *Water*: the surface rises and falls in travelling waves and glints. The waves are done in the shader, on the top corners of the water cubes, so the surface stays in one piece from cube to cube.
  - *Wind*: living tree tops move in any air, with whoever sits on them; once the windmill turns, grass and flowers ripple gently in waves that travel west. Done in the shader that places the cubes, so it costs nothing per cube.
  - *Flowers, mushrooms, apples*: flowers shut at night; the tall mushrooms bounce when knocked; a knock on a fruit tree brings leaves down and, once the bees have set them, an apple, which somebody near by comes and eats.
  - *Furniture*: a swing on the old tree once it is in blossom (a click pushes it, and the squirrel takes turns on it) and a hammock between two palms on the beach (the crab takes to it once the beach is awake).
  - *Diorama camera*: zoomed in close, the camera comes down for a look from the side and the top and bottom of the picture go soft.
  - *Opening*: the world puts itself together when the page loads, from the middle outwards. While the game itself is still on its way there is already an island to look at: a few sand tiles popping into place, in plain HTML and CSS (`#boot` in `game/index.html`), there from the first paint. The game fades them out once its own first frame is drawn and its own island goes on from there. It is a stand-in, not the island that follows, and it never holds the game back: with everything already in the browser it is gone before it has finished.
  - *Ending*: when the whole world is awake, whoever is in the balloon has a few things to say, and they roll up over the island like the credits of a film: ten short paragraphs down the middle of the picture, each on a card of the page's colour so that it reads over land and water alike, for about eighty seconds, with fireworks from about where things got better (`epilogue` in `Island.ts`, `.credits` on the page). The balloon comes once whatever was still being said has been said (after twenty seconds of that at most; what is left goes into the log unsaid), and starts on its piece a second and a half after it is in sight, with the first line already in the picture. The subtitle and the card keep out of its way while it rolls; when the last line has gone off the top it is over by itself (the page says so, since the game's clock stands still while nobody is looking and the words do not), and the card comes up: all but solid, with everybody who is up along its top, the four counts as tiles, and what the game noticed. The space bar, or a click on the balloon, cuts it short; and once it is over, a click on the balloon rolls it again from the top. A finished island that is only being put back skips straight to the card.
  - *Subtitles*: what the game says comes up a line at a time across the bottom of the picture, on a soft patch of the page's colour. Each line stays for as long as it takes somebody to read it who is also watching an island (by its length), then fades; one said while another is up waits its turn, with a breath between them, and is never hurried. The space bar is "read, next", and a small mark in the corner of the patch says another line is waiting (in the corner and not at the end of the words: there the line wrapped afresh whenever the mark came or went, and seemed to change while it was being read); the line itself lets clicks through to the world. Whoever or whatever a line is about is written in its own colour wherever the line names it (bees in yellow, the river in blue, and the word "secret" in gold: `naming` and `HUES` in `GamePage.tsx`), and a click on that word goes to see it. The game speaks when the player does something, and at the big moments. It does not talk by itself: a wish is told by the bubble over the animal's head (a click on it says it) and goes into the log without a word, a bottle on the beach only clinks, and nobody offers a tip that was not asked for. One thing found is one line: what it sets off within a few seconds (the garden the bees fly off to, the dam the beaver takes apart, the mountain the geyser shakes loose) is part of that line and has none of its own, since a second line would only come up long after what it is about. What a click brings about is said at once, except when it finds something: then the picture has a breath to itself (`breath` in `Island.ts`, 2.4 seconds: a thing found, a wish come true; not a secret or a combo, for which nothing much moves, so that their line comes at once), the line that was up goes, and what there is to say comes when the show is over, so that nobody has to choose between reading and looking. The line it replaces does not come back (it used to, after the new one, and by then it was about something long past): it is in the log, and if it was cut off before half its time was up, the journal's button shows a dot for it, unless what cut it off is news of the same thing or the same somebody. And a line that is still waiting and says what to do, or how nearly a thing is done, is let go if its turn only comes round once that is done: a tip for a thing found meanwhile, a riddle for a secret found, a wish granted, "one more push will tip it" after it has tipped (`moot` in `Island.ts`). On a narrow page the land keeps clear of the line; on a wide one the line lies over the bottom of the picture. A game being put back from a save does not talk its way through it. Every line that comes up is also kept on the map, under *Log*.

How the code is split:

- `Island.ts` is the game itself: tiles, water, waking ground, animals' habits, camera and input. `advance` is one step of the game with nothing of how it is shown, which is what lets a saved game be replayed in a fraction of a second; `update` is that plus the drawing.
- `isles/` has one file per isle: its map (one character per tile), its heights, its colours, and what stands on it. Change a map to change the level; add a file and one line in `Island.ts` to add an isle.
- `combos.ts`, `wishes.ts` and `music.ts` are the extras above. The isles leave notes in `memo` when something happens; the combos watch the notes.
- `grid.ts` holds what they share (and the dealing of a seeded island), `cubes.ts` the pools of cubes everything is made of, `critters.ts` the animals.

In the dev server the running game is on `window.island`, handy for trying a trigger from the console. `npm test` also checks that the isle maps fit the grid and do not overlap, that every further map of an isle keeps the coastline and the things of the first and whatever else its story needs (where a bridge lands, what plugs a spring, that still water keeps off the coast), and that dealing an island from a seed only moves what is loose. And it plays whole games without a browser (`test/play.test.ts`), on enough islands for every map of every isle to be played: everything gets found, every isle comes up, every bed ends up wet and every tile wakes. After drawing a map, `node test/play.test.ts 300` plays the first 300 islands, which takes about half a minute. The same file checks the camera: zoomed in on the boulder, a knock sends the water off and the camera widens with it, goes to see the beach that has come up meanwhile, and stays there; a hand on the view ends the following; it goes along with the bird let out of its cage and ends up over the meadow; and a close view keeps its distance when the window is made another shape, with the same nearest distance in a window of any shape. It checks the keys too: + and − zoom on the middle of the view, a key pressed with ⌘ does nothing, and none is held once the window loses the keyboard. And what is said: lines wait their turn, a click is answered at once, the space bar moves on, and every line is kept with who or what it was about; on every island played, a line of the ending is caught being said, stays up for as long as any other line, and moves on at the space bar. And the animals: those with a head have their eyes in it, nobody shut in or asleep fidgets, those that are out find things to do, and they are down for the night within seconds of it falling; every island has its swing, its hammock and six signs, and a knock on one of those counts for nothing.

Keeping it smooth, none of which changes the picture on a machine that keeps up:

- The page draws the frames itself (`Stage` in `GamePage.tsx`): on a screen that redraws more than a hundred times a second it takes every other frame, so the most it draws is a little over sixty a second.
- The same loop keeps count. Under forty-two frames a second for four seconds, the picture gets lighter a step at a time: first the soft shading in the corners at half size, then no more pixels than the page has. If even the lightest made no real difference (a screen held to thirty frames to save its battery), everything goes back on. In the dev server `tier(1)` and `tier(2)` set a step by hand.
- The lamps of the night (the lighthouse, the fireflies) are not there to be drawn while none of them is lit: a lamp is worked out for every pixel, lit or not. The shaders that include them are put together in the background at the start, so nightfall does not stall.
- Only the cubes that changed go to the graphics card each frame, not the whole list of sixteen thousand; particles are sent only while there are any.
- On a touch screen the world is asked what is under the finger when it is tapped, not on every frame.
- The half-size shading needs a small workaround in `Stage`: n8ao 2.0.1 and three 0.187 disagree about what a buffer with two textures answers to `texture`.

## Publishing

The site lives at `https://tiagojeronimo.github.io/`, with the game at `/game/`. It is the repo `tiagojeronimo.github.io` on GitHub: a repo of exactly that name (the account's name, then `.github.io`) is served at the root of that address, which is what every path in the site counts on.

A push to `main` publishes it: `.github/workflows/publish.yml` runs the linter, the tests and the build, and hands `dist` to GitHub Pages. One setting goes with it, once: in the repo's Settings → Pages, *Source* is *GitHub Actions*. On a free account the repo has to be public for Pages to serve it.
